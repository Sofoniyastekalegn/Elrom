Ethiopia grows 100% Coffea arabica, traditionally categorized by growing region (which defines the coffee's commercial "type" or profile) alongside thousands of native heirloom/landrace varieties and Gesha.

Major Growing Regions ("Types" of Ethiopian Coffee)

• Yirgacheffe: Floral, citrusy, and tea-like with a light-to-medium body and bright acidity.
• Sidama (Sidamo): Fruity, sweet, and balanced with floral, berry, or chocolate undertones.
• Guji: Sweet, floral, and fruity (often berry-forward, especially natural/dry-processed lots).
• Harrar (Harar): Bold, heavy-bodied, and dry-processed with winey, wild berry, spice, or mocha notes.
• Limu: Mild, smooth, and balanced with sweet, spicy, or floral notes.
• Jimma (Djimmah): Hearty, full-bodied, and often fruity or earthy.
• Lekempti (Nekemte) & Gimbi (Ghimbi): Western coffees known for full body, sharp acidity, and fruity or wine-like notes.
• Teppi & Bebeka: Southwestern origins with lighter acidity and distinct forest/spice characteristics.
• Kaffa & Illubabor: Historic southwestern regions tied to wild and forest coffee origins.

Varieties & Processing Styles

• Varieties: Mostly grouped as Ethiopian Heirloom or landraces (thousands of indigenous varieties), plus specific research selections (such as Jimma selections) and Gesha (Geisha), which originated in Ethiopia.
• Processing: Washed (cleaner, brighter, floral/citrus) and Natural / Dry-processed (sweeter, heavier body, berry/fruit-forward), with occasional Honey lots.
