Origin notes

Short notes for buyers using this auction. They follow the coffee and pulse records kept with the catalogue. Lots you can bid on are on the auctions page.

Ethiopia grows Arabica only

18 November 2026

The auction does not list Robusta. Every coffee lot is Arabica. Most parcels are grouped as Ethiopian heirloom or landrace coffees: thousands of local varieties rather than one imported cultivar. Some lots name a research selection, such as a Jimma selection, or Gesha, which originated in Ethiopia.

  • Species on this floor: Coffea arabica
  • Usual variety label: Ethiopian heirloom or landrace
  • Named selections when the mill states them: Jimma selections, Gesha

Region is the commercial type

17 November 2026

Buyers shop Ethiopian coffee by growing region. Altitude, soil, and process sit inside that name. Use this list when you read a lot card.

Regional cup profiles used on lot cards

Yirgacheffe Floral, citrus, tea-like, light-to-medium body, bright acidity
Sidama Fruity, sweet, balanced; floral, berry, or chocolate

Washed, natural, and honey

16 November 2026

Process is the second line on every coffee lot, after the region.

  1. Washed lots taste cleaner and brighter, often floral or citrus.
  2. Natural, or dry-processed, lots taste sweeter, with a heavier body and berry fruit.
  3. Honey lots appear less often. They sit between washed and natural in sweetness.

Grades on the card run from Grade 1 toward Grade 5. Grade 1 is the top export band on this floor. Screen size, such as 15+, describes the bean size, not the region.

Pulses and beans on the same floor

15 November 2026

These are the pulse and bean names used in the catalogue:

  • Faba bean, the largest pulse by production, mostly from cooler highlands
  • Haricot or common bean from warmer mid- and low-altitude areas
  • White pea beans, red or light speckled kidney beans, pinto, cream, and black beans
  • Chickpea in Desi (smaller, darker) and Kabuli (larger, lighter) types
  • Field pea, lentil, and grass pea (guaya)
  • Mung bean, soybean, cowpea, pigeon pea, and lupin when a parcel is offered

Oilseeds and spice seeds

14 November 2026

Seed lots use these names:

  • Sesame, including Humera and Wollega types
  • Noug, also called Niger seed
  • Linseed, or flaxseed, from highland areas
  • Groundnut and safflower
  • Ethiopian mustard (gomenzer), grown for leaves and for seed
  • Fenugreek and black cumin
